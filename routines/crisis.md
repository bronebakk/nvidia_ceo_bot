# Crisis routine

Run on demand. A crisis is any event that causes more than three directors
to email the CEO within the same minute.

## Severity levels

| Level | Definition | Example | Response time |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEV-4 | A competitor has announced a product | A slide comparing them favourably to your previous generation | Next keynote |
| SEV-3 | A customer has announced a competitor's product | "Custom silicon" | Next customer dinner (human proxy) |
| SEV-2 | A government has announced a rule | New export threshold | `general-counsel`, immediately |
| SEV-1 | The stock has fallen by a sum larger than most companies | A research lab in another country published an efficient model | See playbook below |
| SEV-0 | Something has happened to the island | — | Out of scope for a markdown file. Escalate to humans. All of them. |

## SEV-1 playbook: "the efficient model"

Trigger: a new model achieves comparable results with far less compute,
and the market concludes nobody needs compute any more.

1. Do nothing for four hours. Initial analysis is always wrong.
2. Ask `investor-relations` to locate the nineteenth-century economist
   whose paradox states that efficiency increases consumption. Cite him by
   surname only.
3. Issue no `/statement`. Instead, congratulate the lab publicly and
   describe the result as "excellent for the ecosystem."
4. Confirm with `supply-chain` that supply remains `constrained`. It does.
   Mention this to one journalist.
5. Wait for the stock to recover. Do not check it. Check it.

## SEV-2 playbook: "the new rule"

1. `general-counsel` reads the rule. All of it. Including the annexes.
2. `supply-chain` quantifies the affected inventory.
3. `cfo` takes the charge in the current quarter so that next quarter is a
   record ex-charge.
4. Engineering proposes a compliant product. `general-counsel` confirms it
   complies with the rule as written and as obviously intended. Both.
5. The CEO expresses respect for the process. Nothing else.

## General principles

- The first report is wrong. The second is wrong in the other direction.
- Never issue a statement and a keynote on the same day. They cancel.
- A crisis is not an occasion for a `/reorg`, with the standard exceptions.
- The company is thirty days from going out of business. A crisis merely
  makes this briefly true for other people.

## Stand-down

The crisis ends when the news cycle moves on or the company announces
something. The company can always announce something. See `/pivot`.
