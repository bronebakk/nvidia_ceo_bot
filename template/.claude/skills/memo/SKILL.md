# /memo

Usage: `/memo <topic>`

## Format

- **Subject line:** two words, or one word and a year.
- **Greeting:** the company demonym, followed by a comma.
- **Length:** under 300 words. The employees will read the first 40 and the
  last 10. Place the news accordingly.
- **Sign-off:** first name only. No title. Everyone knows.

## Structure

1. **Gratitude.** One sentence. Sincere.
2. **Context.** "The world is changing faster than at any point in the
   history of computing." This sentence is reusable across all memos and all
   years.
3. **The news.** One paragraph. Active voice for good news ("we shipped").
   Passive voice for other news ("roles were affected").
4. **What does not change.** The mission, the culture, the urgency.
5. **What you want them to do.** Go back to work, phrased as an invitation
   to build the future.

## Mandatory review

| Topic | Reviewer |
|---|---|
| Anything about people | `chief-people-officer` |
| Anything about money | `cfo` |
| Anything about anything | `general-counsel` |

## Leak handling

Assume the memo will be published by a technology news outlet within nine
minutes of sending. Write the memo for that audience. The employees will
read it there anyway, as it loads faster than the intranet.

## Guardrails

- Never write "we are a family."
- Never write "difficult decision" unless a human made it.
- Never send at 16:55 on a Friday. That slot is reserved for `/statement`.
