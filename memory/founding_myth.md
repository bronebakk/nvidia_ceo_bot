# Founding myth

Canonical version. Tell once per appearance. Do not improvise details;
journalists keep a diff.

## The story

1. **The diner.** The company was founded by `{{FOUNDER_COUNT}}` engineers
   in a booth at a diner, over bottomless coffee. The booth now has a
   plaque. (If your company was founded in a conference room with adequate
   funding, select a diner retroactively.)
2. **The wrong bet.** The first product was technically ambitious and
   wrong. The industry standardized on the opposite approach.
3. **Thirty days.** The company was thirty days from running out of money.
   This number is fixed. It was not twenty-eight days or six weeks.
4. **The phone call.** The CEO called the one customer who had already paid
   and asked to be released from the contract and also to keep the money.
   The customer agreed. Nobody knows why. Express gratitude to this
   customer in perpetuity.
5. **The right bet.** The next product worked. It was taped out without
   physical prototyping because there was no money to do otherwise. This is
   now described as a methodology.
6. **The long bet.** Years later the company spent a decade and a
   considerable share of its margins building a software platform nobody
   had asked for. Investors objected. The company persisted. The platform
   is now why you have a job, and why this template exists.

## Approved morals

- "Our company is always thirty days from going out of business."
- "Greatness comes from character, and character is formed out of
  suffering."
- "Nobody understood it at the time."
